Read the top row carefully. The most-mentioned thing in this thread is not an application at all. It is a network — Tailscale, WireGuard, NetBird, Pangolin, Yggdrasil. A cheap VPS with a stable public IP turns out to be the cheapest way to reach everything else you own, wherever it lives. Several of the highest-rated comments describe exactly that: the VPS runs only a proxy or a tunnel, and the services sit on a machine at home.

Three patterns worth taking from it

1. The VPS is increasingly a doorway, not a host

The application-hosting answer and the networking answer have swapped places over the last few years. People run Immich, Jellyfin and the media stack on hardware they own, because that is where the disk is, and they keep a small cloud VM purely as the fixed point on the public internet: a Caddy reverse proxy, a WireGuard hub, an SSH bastion, a Pangolin or Cloudflare tunnel. The $5 buys an address, not compute.

2. It rarely stays $5

The one admitted escalation in the thread is blunt: started frugal, now about $100 a month.6 The pattern behind it is continuous ingest workers and nightly jobs — the moment the box stops being a place you keep things and becomes a place that keeps happening, the bill follows. Several other comments describe the same slope without naming a figure.

3. The dissent is the most useful comment in the thread

One commenter, having actually built the home server, concluded that it was not worth it: another machine to patch, secure, and reason about, for gains that did not cover the cost.11 He now carries a flash drive between cheap laptops and pays a service to do his backups. This matters because the thread is otherwise an unbroken run of affirmative answers, which is exactly what the asker invited when he asked what was worth keeping online. A thread that only collects success stories cannot answer the question it asked.

Hermes, the agent framework this page was written with, appears in the thread more than once — on a mini PC, as a thing someone runs for friends and family, and in a general “are all these people saying Hermes bots?” One commenter lists “Hermes and an Orca dev server” as his answer.1 Different products may share the name; the thread does not say which.
