The short answer

A healthy, correctly maintained Sonet does not need a special service, engine modification, or scheduled engine-cooling stops merely for a 300 km day. That is a planning judgment, not a remote inspection. Your bigger practical risks are tyre condition, an unresolved fault, and a tired return drive.

At 4,500 km: you are beyond Kia’s initial 1,000 km break-in precautions. Kia notes that economy, performance and oil consumption may still settle up to approximately 6,000 km. There is no need to keep a permanent break-in RPM limit or wait until 10,000 km to travel. Check the oil level correctly and drive normally, smoothly.1013

Go, provided these are true

  1. Service history is current by both distance and elapsed time.
  2. Tyres, spare, fluids, brakes and lights check out; no unresolved warnings.
  3. You are rested and can change the return plan if tired.

Fix first or postpone

Persistent warning lights, repeated loss of pressure, a tyre bulge/cut, abnormal braking or steering, fluid leakage, overheating, or significant shudder/loss of power are not issues to “test on the highway.” Get them checked before departure.1970

During each 150 km leg

Drive for the conditions

  1. Obey the posted limit; slow further for villages, intersections, rain, animals and poor visibility.
  2. Maintain generous following distance; increase it in rain and behind vehicles blocking your view.
  3. Overtake only with a clear sightline and enough room. Do not rely on turbo acceleration to rescue a marginal gap.
  4. Keep ESC on for normal road use; cruise control, if fitted, is for suitable clear conditions—not fatigue management.

These are conservative planning recommendations, not a route-specific speed prescription. Kia emphasises reduced speed and extra spacing in rain and darkness.19

Stop for you, not on an engine timer

Plan a safe 15-minute break by about two hours of driving; stop earlier whenever needed. This is driver-fatigue guidance from the UK Highway Code, not an Indian statutory break rule or a Kia cooling interval.48

Use a proper fuel station, restaurant or rest area—not the carriageway or a highway shoulder for a routine break. Stretch, drink water, use the toilet and reassess alertness. Yawning, wandering attention or missed signs mean stop safely; do not wait for the planned break.48

Turbo petrol: what to do at a stop

After high-speed or extended driving under heavy engine load, Kia says to idle for about one minute before switching off. Do not blip the throttle before shutdown. This is not a requirement to idle for several minutes after every short, gentle drive, nor to stop periodically merely to cool the engine.7

Exception: an overheating/steam warning is an emergency procedure, not a normal turbo cooldown. Follow the warning guidance and call for help; do not blindly keep an overheating engine running.70

If your Sonet has the 7-speed DCT

On a slope, hold the car with the foot/parking brake, never the accelerator. Minimise repeated creeping and hard launches, especially on inclines. Leave a sensible gap and move smoothly rather than inching continuously. Kia’s manual suggests N or P when stationary in stop-and-go traffic; fully stop and secure the car before choosing P. Do not repeatedly shuffle gears while still rolling.1768

If the display says “Transmission Hot! Park with engine on”, stop safely, secure the car, select P and follow the indicated cooling instructions with the engine running. Resume only when the car says it is cooled; persistent warnings require Kia service. This DCT instruction is not the same as the engine-overheating procedure.1770

If your Sonet has iMT / if the route has hills

Do not apply DCT-only advice to an iMT. Follow your gearbox’s own instructions and warnings. In either case, do not hold the car on a hill with throttle. On a descent, use an appropriate lower gear/engine braking and avoid continuously riding the brakes; never coast in neutral. Choose a lower gear rather than forcing a high gear and heavy throttle at very low RPM. Exact shift speeds depend on conditions, not a fixed internet RPM rule.1117

Tyre pressure rises while driving—do not “correct” hot tyres down

Kia explicitly says not to release air from warm tyres to reach the cold-pressure figure. Check and adjust when cold; investigate a warning or a substantial loss of pressure rather than dismissing it as heat. Do not copy another owner’s 32/35/38 PSI setting.21

A realistic same-day structure

Illustrative planning model, not a route ETA: at an average moving speed of 50 km/h, each 150 km leg takes three hours. Add a 15-minute break per leg: approximately 6½ hours for the round-trip driving and breaks, before your visit, meals, city traffic or delays. Never increase road speed to make this example true.

07:00 — depart rested. Gentle first kilometres; no rush through the city exit.

08:30–08:45 — planned safe break. Earlier is fine; stop when needed.

About 10:15 — arrive. Appropriate turbo cooldown if the preceding drive was under heavy load; park securely.

Before return — take a quiet rest and reassess. Confirm fuel, alertness, weather and the route. Look for tyre damage or leaks; do not bleed warm tyres. Change the plan if tired.

Illustrative return 13:30–16:45, with a break around 15:00. Adjust to your actual route and local sunset. If your visit cannot fit comfortably, start later/return differently or stay overnight—not a faster drive.

Fuel: plan conservatively

These are arithmetic scenarios, not promised Sonet mileage: 300 km needs 25.0 litres at 12 km/l, 20.0 litres at 15 km/l, or 16.7 litres at 18 km/l. Use your own observed consumption and add headroom for traffic, diversions and reserve. Start with ample fuel; do not plan to arrive on an almost empty tank. Owner dashboard figures are not a range guarantee.4

After arriving home: note any warning, pressure loss, vibration or new noise. Recheck pressure cold the next morning if needed. A normal uneventful trip does not automatically require an oil change or special post-trip service; follow the actual maintenance schedule.6

If something goes wrong

First priority: keep people out of traffic. Ease off, signal and reach a safe place if the car can still be controlled. Use hazards once stopped. Avoid stopping on a blind bend or narrow shoulder if a safer refuge is reachable. If safe to exit, use the side away from traffic and wait clear of the road/behind a barrier. Never stand between a stopped car and approaching traffic or attempt repairs exposed to traffic.

Stop and get help

  • Overheating/steam: stop safely; follow the manual. Never open a hot coolant pressure cap. Visible steam or coolant loss is not a “keep going” situation.70
  • Tyre failure or severe vibration: keep control, slow progressively and stop safely. Do not attempt a highway-side jack job unless the site is genuinely protected and you know the procedure.
  • A red oil-pressure/brake warning, loss of braking, strong fuel smell or loss of power: do not continue simply because home is close. Ask roadside assistance for next steps.

What not to over-prepare

  1. No forced service at 4,500 km solely for 300 km. Fix overdue service or symptoms instead.6
  2. No five-to-ten-minute morning warm-up. Brief settling, then moderate driving.7
  3. No generic “highway PSI,” hot-tyre deflation or premium-fuel requirement. Your placard and fuel specification decide.921
  4. No engine-cooling break every hour. Plan breaks for alertness; use the specified turbo shutdown precaution after heavy-load driving.748
  5. No new accessory shopping spree. Know your spare, tools, coverage and safe stops first.
